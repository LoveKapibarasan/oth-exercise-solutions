15.3Commitment Protocol
A cryptographic commitment scheme allows one party to ”commit” to a chosen value (or statement)
while keeping it hidden from others, with the ability to reveal the committed value later. It is designed
to be bothhiding(the receiver cannot know the value before it’s revealed) andbinding(the sender cannot
change the value after committing to it).
For a coin flip over the telephone, Mallory proposes the following protocol:

• Alice and Mallory agree on an odd bit widthkfor the numbers used in the protocol (e.g.,k=129).
• Alice chooses a random bitb a ∈ {0, 1}; Mallory chooses a random bitbm ∈ {0, 1}.
•Alice’s Commitment:She chooses two numbersx a,y a with 2k−1 ≤x a ≤y a ≤2 k −1, such that the
middle bit of the binary representation ofx a has the valueb a. Alice sendsn a =x a ·y a to Mallory.
•Mallory’s Commitment:She chooses two numbersx m,y m with 2k−1 ≤x m ≤y m ≤2 k −1, such
that the middle bit of the binary representation ofxm has the valueb m. Mallory sendsn m =x m ·y m
to Alice.
• Alice reveals her commitment by sending(x a,y a)to Mallory.
• Mallory reveals her commitment by sending(x m,y m)to Alice.
• Alice verifies that 2 k−1 ≤x m ≤y m ≤2 k −1 andn m =x m ·y m.
• Mallory verifies that 2 k−1 ≤x a ≤y a ≤2 k −1 andn a =x a ·y a.
• The result of the coin flip is the bitb= (b a +b m)mod 2.
(a) How can Mallory manipulate the result of the coin flip? Demonstrate that your idea works by
writing a short script to simulate it.
(b) Modify the protocol so that neither Alice nor Mallory has sole control over the outcome of the coin
flip.
Solution:
(a) If Alice chooses the numbersx a andy a cleverly (e.g., as prime numbers), they cannot be
factorized by Mallory. Thus, Mallory cannot determineb a beforehand.
However, Mallory can choose four prime numbersa,b,c,deach with a bit length of approxi-
mately≈k/2, such that the following holds:
•x 1 :=a·b,y 1 :=c·d,x 2 :=a·c,y 2 :=b·dall have binary representations of lengthk.
•x 1 ≤y 1 andx 2 ≤y 2.
•x 1 andx 2 have different middle bits.
To force the outcomeb∈ {0, 1}of the coin flip, Mallory simply waits until she receives the pair
(xa,y a)from Alice. From this, she calculatesb a and then the required bitbm := (b+b a)mod 2.
If the middle bit ofx 1 has the valueb m, Mallory sends the factorization(x 1,y 1). Otherwise,
the middle bit ofx 2 has the valueb m, and Mallory sends(x 2,y 2).
Sincea,b,c,dare large prime numbers, Alice cannot efficiently factorize the numbern m =
a·b·c·dsent by Mallory, so she accepts whichever factorization Mallory reveals.
